HOW THIS SOFTWARE IS VERIFIED

The UTM zone constants are not taken on trust. UTM is defined by constants
every zone shares - scale 0.9996 on the central meridian, a false easting of
500,000 m, a false northing of 0 m at the equator - and by its central
meridian, 6z - 183 degrees for zone z. This software stores those and derives
the rest; its test suite holds each stored zone to them.

The conversion itself is checked against the National Geodetic Survey's own
Coordinate Conversion and Transformation Tool (NCAT). Thirty-four positions in
UTM zone 16 were converted by beta NCAT in NATRF2022 (captured 2026-10-22),
and those results are held in the test suite as fixed reference values. This
software reproduces their northing, easting, convergence and scale factor
within the precision NCAT prints converting to the grid; converting from it,
the position and convergence within that precision, and the scale factor
within 0.0008 ppm of the value NCAT prints.

The equations do not depend on the frame: a UTM zone on NATRF2022 has exactly
the constants of the same zone on NAD83(2011), so the two hundred twelve
production NCAT positions in NAD83(2011) zones 16 and 17 check the same
equations. Zone 15, which covers only the western tip of Gogebic County, has
no NCAT position on either frame; it is the same projection with its central
meridian six degrees west of zone 16's, and the suite holds it to zone 16 at
points the same distance from their meridians.

The NATRF2022 anchors come from NGS's beta products, published ahead of the
official release for implementation. They are to be re-frozen against NGS's
official release when it is published, and the difference measured
(docs/REFREEZE-NSRS.md).

The NCAT checks compare this software against NGS, not against itself.
